# 07 — Behavioral & Leadership (STAR format)

**STAR = Situation (15%) → Task (10%) → Action (55%, use "I", not "we") → Result (20%, with numbers).** Keep it to 2 minutes.

> ✍️ **Fill in your real stories below.** Templates are given; replace the [brackets] with your experience. Bring them to a practice session and I'll help you polish them.

---

### Q1. "Tell me about yourself." (2 minutes)
**Template:**
"I'm Deepchand, a full-stack engineer with 9+ years of experience, mainly in **Node.js, React, and SQL** with Sequelize, and Docker-based deployments.
Currently at [company], I [lead/build] [product, e.g. the NIELIT MIS portal] which serves [X users / Y institutes]. My key contributions: [1. architecture/scale win with a number], [2. performance win, e.g. cut report generation from 40s to 3s], [3. leadership: mentoring N developers, code-review standards].
Recently I've been using **AI-assisted development with Claude Code** to speed up delivery and code reviews, which [result].
I'm now looking for a [Senior/Lead] role where I can own the backend architecture at a larger scale — which is why [company]'s work on [X] excites me."

---

### Q2. Your most challenging project / most complex system you built.
Structure: business context → architecture (draw it) → the hardest technical problem → your options → your decision and why → measurable result → what you'd do differently.

### Q3. A production incident you handled.
Show: calm triage → mitigate first (rollback / feature flag) → root cause → permanent fix → **blameless postmortem** + prevention (alerts, tests).
*Story:* [e.g. DB connection pool exhaustion during result-declaration traffic → …]

### Q4. Conflict with a teammate / disagreement on a technical decision.
Show: listening first, data-driven discussion (benchmark/POC), disagree-and-commit, the relationship preserved.

### Q5. Disagreement with your manager or product owner.
Show: respectful push-back with trade-offs (scope vs timeline vs quality), offering alternatives, alignment on business goals.

### Q6. A failure or mistake you made.
Own it genuinely, what you learned, and the process change you introduced. Don't pick a fake weakness.

### Q7. How do you mentor junior developers?
Pair programming, code-review guidelines, giving ownership of small features, weekly 1:1s, documentation. Example: [junior X grew to handle module Y independently].

### Q8. Tight deadline — how did you deliver?
Prioritization (MoSCoW), cutting scope rather than quality, communicating risk early, parallelizing the team.

### Q9. How do you ensure code quality in your team?
PR templates, mandatory reviews, linting + formatting in CI, unit/integration test coverage targets, architecture decision records (ADRs), AI-assisted review (Claude Code) as a first pass.

### Q10. Technical debt — how do you handle it?
Make it visible (a backlog with impact), allocate ~15–20% per sprint, tie refactors to features, measure (bug rate, lead time).

### Q11. Why are you leaving your current company?
Positive and forward-looking: "I've grown a lot at [X] and delivered [Y]. I'm looking for larger scale / product challenges / a bigger ownership role." Never criticize your current employer.

### Q12. Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?
"Growing as a technical leader — owning architecture for critical systems and mentoring engineers, either as a Staff Engineer or an Engineering Manager, depending on where I add the most value."

### Q13. Why should we hire you?
Map 3 of your strengths to 3 things in their job description, with proof for each.

### Q14. Questions YOU should ask the interviewer
- "What does success look like in the first 90 days for this role?"
- "What's the biggest technical challenge the team is facing right now?"
- "How are architectural decisions made here?"
- "How do you handle on-call and production incidents?"
- "What's the team structure and how does it interact with product?"

## Salary negotiation tips
- Don't give a number first if you can avoid it: "I'm looking for a fair package for a senior role; what's the budgeted range?"
- If you're pushed, give a **range** whose lower end is your real target. Base it on market data (AmbitionBox, Glassdoor, Levels.fyi).
- Negotiate the total package: base, joining bonus (to cover a notice-period buyout), variable pay, ESOPs, remote work.
- Use competing offers politely: "I have another offer at X; I prefer your company — can you match it?"
- Get everything in writing before resigning.
